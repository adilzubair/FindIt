This is what an old drive looks like to a computer: IMG_4136.jpg, IMG_4213.jpg, a video called City break. FindIt lets you search it the way you remember things. Ask it something. The search really runs, here, on your device.

    01 What just happened

    You searched by memory, not by filename.

    1. It looked at the pictures.

      Every photo and every second of video was turned into numbers that describe what's in it. Your words are turned into the same kind of numbers, and the closest ones win. That is how “dog running on the beach” finds IMG_4136.jpg.

    2. It found the moment.

      Videos are sampled as stills, so a match points at a time, not a file. Safari day 3 opens at 0:04, where the elephants are.

    3. It read the words, and what they mean.

      “AB-0091” finds the invoice by its exact words. “Can I keep a pet” finds the lease's pets clause, which never says “keep”.

        03 Where it lives

        In the app, everything it knows is on the drive.

        The program, its models, the index, previews, transcripts and people data all sit in one folder beside your files. Your folders stay exactly where they are, and FindIt only ever reads them.

        That's what makes it portable. Prepare the drive once on a capable PC, then plug it into any Windows PC and search. Nothing to install, nothing to sign in to, no internet.

        1. 1

          Prepare once

          Choose the folders to make searchable. A large library can take hours; pause whenever you like, and what's done is searchable along the way.

        2. 2

          Eject and carry it

          The index travels with the files. Next time, only what changed is read again.

        3. 3

          Search anywhere

          Open FindIt.exe on any Windows 10 or 11 PC. Searching works on modest hardware.

        On the host PC, FindIt leaves only disposable caches in %LOCALAPPDATA%\FindIt: a copy of the program so the drive can be ejected cleanly, a note of which models were already verified, and tiny process records with no filenames or content.

        04 Promises

        What it will never do with your files.

        1. Never change them.

          Originals are never modified, moved or renamed. On a physical test drive, updating FindIt in place left every existing file identical by SHA-256, length and modification time.

          Drive validation

        2. Never send them anywhere.

          No telemetry, no accounts and no background network access. Every model runs on the PC in front of you, just as the demo above runs in your browser.

          Architecture

        3. Never lose the index.

          Preparation writes checksummed generations and switches over only when one is complete. Twenty-five recovery scenarios, from killed processes to a full disk, keep the previous index intact.

          Recovery validation

        4. Never pretend to be sure.

          Every result says why it matched. People results are labelled as possible matches until you confirm who is who.

        05 Status

        An honest preview.

        FindIt works end to end and is covered by over 200 automated tests and physical-drive checks. It has not passed its public-release gates yet.

        Verified

        • Exact and semantic search, timestamped speech and video, and named-person evidence, on a physical FAT32 USB stick
        • A prepared drive opened and searched on a second PC
        • 25 crash and corruption recovery scenarios with the real worker
        • Native index recall of 97–100% at 100k records, warm P95 under 150 ms

        Still to prove

        • Clean, offline Windows 10 and 11 machines with no runtimes installed
        • One million files on an 8 GB, CPU-only laptop
        • Relevance on representative held-out queries, Hit@10 of 85% or better
        • Code-signed packages

        06 Get it

        Put it on a drive.

        1. Download and check it

          Compare the hash with the published checksum.

          Get-FileHash .\FindIt-0.1.0-preview.9-win-x64.zip

        2. Extract it to the root of the drive

          So FindIt.exe and a FindIt folder sit beside your own folders. Allow about 2 GB plus room for the index; NTFS, exFAT and FAT32 all work.

        3. Prepare, eject, carry

          Open FindIt.exe, choose folders under Drive, and start. Use Eject before unplugging.
